# Hydration methodology — draft v1

Reviewed 2026-11-01. This is an engineering prototype, not a clinically validated algorithm or a medical prescription. It is not yet connected to the user interface, profile, reminders, or database. Do not deploy as clinical guidance without appropriate professional review.

## Evidence and scope

- National Academies, *Dietary Reference Intakes for Water* (2005), https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10925/chapter/6: adequate intakes describe **total water**, including food and all beverages, for population groups. They are not individualized plain-water requirements. The 80/20 food/beverage split is a population observation, not a measurement for every person (https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/report-sets-dietary-intake-levels-for-water-salt-and-potassium-to-maintain-health-and-reduce-chronic-disease-risk).
- EFSA, *Scientific Opinion on Dietary Reference Values for water* (2010), https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/1459: total water includes food moisture, drinking water and other beverages. Values assume moderate temperature and physical activity and differ from the National Academies values. They do not provide a universal heat increment or a personal plain-water goal.
- Royal Cornwall Hospitals NHS Trust, *Nutrition and Hydration for Adults Policy* (2025), section 6.2.9, https://doclibrary-rcht.cornwall.nhs.uk/GET/d10368900: describes 30–35 ml/kg/day **oral fluids** in an inpatient care policy, dependent on patient characteristics. Using it as a draft starting range for healthy community adults is an unvalidated extrapolation, not a recommendation endorsed by the source for this application. It is not total water and it does not separate plain water from other beverages.
- ACSM, *Exercise and Fluid Replacement* (2007), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277604/: individualized replacement and pre/post exercise body-mass measurements are useful because sweat losses vary. The optional field calculation is an approximation and assumes comparable weigh-ins. It should not be used to drive an automatic intake increase without appropriate review.
- CDC/NIOSH, *Heat Stress: Hydration* (2017), https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/45851: practical hydration guidance is specific to occupational heat exposure and warns against excessive hourly intake. It does not justify adding a fixed amount to a daily goal solely from a job title.
- WHO, *Heatwaves: How to stay cool*, https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/heatwaves-how-to-stay-cool and CDC, *First Aid for Heat Illness*, https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/138337: severe heat illness is an emergency requiring urgent help and cooling; fluids alone are not treatment. Do not give fluids by mouth if someone cannot safely swallow.

## Algorithm, assumptions, and safety

`src/lib/hydration/estimate.ts` is pure and versioned. It accepts existing age and current weight plus explicit work/activity/heat/exercise context and four nullable safety answers. Safety answers must be complete before producing a number. Under 18, age 65+, or any reported fluid restriction, fluid-balance condition, pregnancy/breastfeeding, or relevant medication results in clinician guidance unless the user supplies their clinician's daily fluid amount. A clinician amount always overrides the general calculation and is interpreted as **all beverages**, not only plain water. No heat, work or exercise amount is added to it.

For other adults the draft starting range is 30–35 ml/kg/day of **all beverages**, rounded to the nearest 100 ml. This is a coarse heuristic based on the hospital policy above, not verified healthy-person guidance. The software declines the heuristic below 40 kg, above 120 kg or when the upper end exceeds 4 L. These are conservative product gates for review, not medically established boundaries or safe limits. Individual needs may be lower or higher, and an upper-bound display is not an instruction to force fluid consumption.

A plain-water range is only calculated when the user supplies their estimated water from other drinks; the amount is subtracted once from the beverage range, floored at zero. Total water is calculated only when the user supplies an estimate of water in food; this is added once to the beverage range. Neither input is an actual measurement, and both estimates may be inaccurate. Do not equate the result with a diagnosis, a mandatory goal or an upper safe limit. A future UI must label food/other-drink inputs, ranges and uncertainty, and distinguish food water from beverage water.

The engine recognizes work, heat, activity and exercise context, explains higher sweat-loss risk and gives electrolyte/heat-safety context, but **does not add unsupported numerical increments** or automatically infer sweating from a job title. It does not yet consume activity records, compute targets over time, track clinician limit consumption, or generate reminders. Significant changes require user review and an auditable history in later phases. The optional exercise sweat estimate is `pre kg − post kg + fluid liters − urine liters`; kilograms are treated approximately as liters of water. The validation bounds and 10 L sanity guard are software checks, not clinical limits.

Example: 70 kg adult, safety questions negative, moderate/cool conditions → estimated **all-beverage** range 2.1–2.5 L/day. If they independently estimate 0.4 L water in other drinks and 0.5 L from food, illustrative plain-water range is 1.7–2.1 L and total-water range 2.6–3.0 L. These are arithmetic illustrations, not individually verified requirements. A hot outdoor worker with the same weight currently receives the same numeric baseline plus heat/sweat cautions; the tool cannot infer their extra losses from job categories alone.

## Status and verification

Synthetic unit checks: `node scripts/verify-hydration-engine.cjs`. Scenarios include office, outdoor, delivery, gym, home, safety flags, clinician override, missing contributions, invalid values and sweat-loss arithmetic. There is no clinical review, UI, persistent hydration profile, schedule, reminder action, weather provider, background push, user history integration, timezone handling or live database testing in this phase. No new dependency, migration or production operation is needed for the standalone module.

## Development UI stage (2026-11-02)

An optional Water tracker panel now uses the standalone draft with the user's saved age and weight and explicit safety/work/heat inputs. Answers and output are held only in browser component state; no profile, target, logs or reminder schedule is written. A user may enter a clinician-prescribed amount for all beverages and an optional estimate for other drinks; it is not treated as a plain-water target. The draft still does not numerically adjust for work, heat or exercise, generate a drinking schedule, or claim clinical validation. Height does not drive a numerical estimate. Earlier statements in this document about no UI describe the earlier standalone phase; this development panel does not imply deployment or professional validation.

## Production release gate

The development panel is disabled in the release build pending independent clinical review of the community extrapolation. The draft source remains available for further work; no personalized numeric hydration suggestion or automatic heat adjustment is enabled. This release does not change saved water targets or turn the draft into a medical recommendation.
