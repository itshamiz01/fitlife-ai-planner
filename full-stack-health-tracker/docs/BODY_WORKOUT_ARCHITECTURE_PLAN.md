# Body assessment and workout transformation: architecture audit and staged plan

Date: 2026-11-01. Status: audit and implementation plan, not a claim that the product is implemented.

## Existing architecture and reuse
- The Next.js app uses PostgreSQL/Drizzle and authenticated owner-scoped records. `wellnest_users` already stores age, height, current/target weight, a gender field, activity, diet, weight goal, exercise experience, coarse equipment, session duration, and four fitness focuses. The gender field is not a reliable proxy for physiological sex; the current energy estimator uses it, including a midpoint for `other`, which is an existing limitation that must not be repeated or silently changed.
- `src/lib/planner.ts` creates seven days with two textual activities per day based on age, broad activity, equipment, experience and fitness focus. It lacks safety screening, chosen days, job workload, structured exercise instructions, dated sessions, and recovery-guided progression. A gentle routine for children/older adults is not a validated pediatric or medical exercise prescription. Current profiles may already have generated workouts with no screening.
- `wellnest_workouts` stores recurring weekday, descriptive text and a single completion boolean, not dated completion, actual exercise sets or history. CRUD is owner-scoped in `api/records`; users can manually edit existing workouts. `fitlife_plan_archives` stores previous meals/workouts with profile snapshots. `archiveAndReplacePlan` replaces *both* meal and workout rows when a planning profile changes or plan refresh is requested. An independent workout planner must not accidentally erase/regenerate diet or overwrite prior manual activities.
- Weight records, manual activity, sleep, and measurement journals exist; generic measurement titles do not provide structured waist/hip/body-fat history. Existing optional pre/post-workout meals are manually selected; nutrition numbers are estimates. Water logs and open-app reminders exist; the safer personalized hydration estimator remains a standalone draft, not an integrated hydration system. Do not advertise the latter as available or increase fluids automatically.
- Coach uses deterministic local responses plus an optional existing Groq intent classifier. It currently builds an unscreened sample workout for adult questions rather than reading a detailed workout plan; completion replies explicitly distinguish current checkmarks from actual dated exercise records. Do not send sensitive safety answers to a provider or replace the provider without approval. Nutrition and existing AI/provider contracts need separate review before integration.
- Existing profile/records/plan routes validate origin and authenticate. Exports, archives, account deletion, demo registration, migration compatibility, public profile allowlists and verification fixtures must be updated deliberately when adding new personal data.

## Evidence and clinical boundary decisions
- WHO 2020 physical activity guidance gives population-level targets of 150–300 moderate or 75–150 vigorous aerobic minutes per week plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. Any activity can be beneficial; this is not an automatic beginner starting load. Older adults benefit from multicomponent strength and balance work. https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/f3885485-e7eb-4504-8026-edd9bb53a6ee/content
- ACSM 2026 position stand summarizes resistance training evidence in healthy adults, emphasizing individualization and benefits of varied and lower-dose approaches; it does not clinically validate any algorithm here. https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2026/04000/american_college_of_sports_medicine_position.21.aspx
- Adult BMI is screening, not a diagnosis or direct measure of fat, distribution, health, or personal ideal weight. CDC applies its adult interpretation from age 20, and children and teens require growth references: https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/about/index.html and https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/faq/index.html. WHO publishes growth reference data for ages 5–19: https://www.who.int/tools/growth-reference-data-for-5to19-years/indicators/bmi-for-age. This initial implementation conservatively withholds categories and ranges below 20 rather than misclassifying ages 18–19; users receive an explanation and referral. This is an implementation boundary, not a claim that 18–19-year-olds are children.
- WHO Expert Consultation, Lancet 2004;363:157–163, DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(03)15268-3: additional public-health action points 23 and 27.5 for Asian populations, not a new universal BMI category or individual diagnosis. Population selection must be voluntary and not inferred from location. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14726171/
- NICE NG246 uses waist-to-height ratio alongside BMI in adults with BMI below 35; screening is not diagnosis and evidence/clinical context matters. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng246/chapter/Rationale-and-impact. Avoid interpreting the ratio outside that scope or implying a low ratio guarantees health.
- Pregnancy/postpartum, illness and other clinical contexts may make general BMI ranges and training advice inappropriate. Do not use a body assessment as medical clearance; do not invent age-, ethnicity-, sex- or occupation-based automatic exercise capabilities.

## Stages and intended files
1. This audit. No application behavior or database changes.
2. A pure versioned body-assessment module plus tests and methodology documentation. Input validation and exact boundary classification before rounding; metric/imperial conversions; optional measurements; no fabricated values; no goal/timeline automation. Initially standalone to avoid presenting assessment where pregnancy/clinical applicability is unknown. UI integration follows safety and usability review.
3. Add an account-owned supplemental fitness profile with explicit unknown/yes/no safety answers and choice of population reference; reuse existing profile values rather than a second user. Unit preference is presentation, not source of truth. Clearly distinguish occupational activity from structured training, movement difficulties from diagnoses, and no-equipment from unspecified. Missing or affirmative safety responses block prescriptions; urgent symptoms require stop and urgent help. Build reversible additive migrations with documented rollback, not yet applied to production. Preserve old profiles and plans.
4. A curated, versioned exercise library with equipment, movement patterns, technique, regressions and contraindication metadata; no invented media. Validate all referenced exercise IDs and required equipment.
5. A deterministic generator with conservative session time budgets, recovery days, experience, equipment and lifestyle constraints; only generate after safety inputs are complete. User selects goals; explain conflicts with existing food goal rather than silently changing diet or weight targets. No guaranteed results or automatic numerical weight milestones without validated safety rules.
6. Add account-owned versioned workout plans, dated sessions, explicit completion/skips, and set-level actual records. Use stable IDs, transaction locks and idempotency; validate all parent-child ownership and avoid duplicating existing logs. Retain historical plans; rescheduling is explicit and never marks missed work complete. Do not reinterpret legacy boolean checkmarks as dated exercise history.
7. Apply progression only after recorded control, effort, pain and recovery feedback; one variable at a time, reversible and explained. Initial conservative rule proposals require review, tests and explicit limitations; no automatic progression through pain.
8. Coach reads only authorized saved plan and actual data, with deterministic safety checks before topic interpretation. The optional provider must not override rules, receive unnecessary health data, or assert unrecorded performance. No provider replacement without authorization.
9. Connect to current diet and hydration through shared actual profile data; show conflicts and existing limitations, avoid invented calorie/protein/hydration targets and do not call the standalone hydration module an active integration.
10. Integrate mobile-friendly views, export and account deletion, privacy controls and migration/rollback instructions. Test synthetic multi-account isolation, safety flags, all profile scenarios, invalid inputs, rounding boundaries, units, DST/date transitions, user-controlled rescheduling, duplicate submissions, migrations and regression of auth, meal, recipe, water and history flows. Real PostgreSQL concurrency and real-device testing remain separate from PGlite.

## Risk and release gates
- Do not imply generated routines are safe for all users; absence of disclosed conditions is not medical clearance. Clinician restrictions win. For concerning exercise-related chest pain, fainting or severe unexplained breathlessness, stop and seek urgent medical help.
- Never use adult BMI categories or weight-loss prescriptions for minors. Withhold the initial assessment engine's adult interpretation from 18–19 as explained above.
- Avoid automatically importing gender as physiological sex, claiming an Asian ideal-weight formula, estimating unmeasured body fat, or assigning workloads based on ethnicity or gender.
- Existing workout recommendations remain unscreened legacy functionality until an integration/migration strategy is designed and tested; do not present them as having passed new safety screening.
- Explicitly report unimplemented phases and unexecuted tests. Never deploy, apply migrations to production, use paid APIs or push without authorization.
